# GreenGrade Independent Advisory Panel — Terms of Reference

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Draft for founding member review
**Issued by:** Consciobite
**Date:** May 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

The GreenGrade Independent Advisory Panel ("the Panel") exists to protect the scientific integrity and public credibility of the GreenGrade scoring methodology. It provides an independent check that commercial relationships between Consciobite and food manufacturers cannot influence how products are scored.

This document is the Panel's founding charter. It sets out who sits on it, what it can and cannot do, and how it reports its findings.

---

## 2. The Problem This Solves

Consciobite charges manufacturers a listing fee to have their products scored on the platform. At the same time, Consciobite claims those scores are independent and scientifically grounded.

That combination creates a structural conflict of interest. It does not mean scores are manipulated — but it creates the reasonable suspicion that they could be. Regulators, retailers, and journalists will raise this, and "trust us" is not a sufficient answer.

The Panel resolves this by separating the commercial relationship (Consciobite ↔ manufacturer) from the scientific one (Panel ↔ methodology). No Panel member may have a commercial relationship with Consciobite or with any listed manufacturer. The Panel has read-only access to scoring logic, audit trails, and commercial arrangements. Its findings are published.

---

## 3. Composition

The Panel has three seats, each representing a distinct type of independence.

### 3.1 Academic Member
An active researcher in food systems, life-cycle assessment, environmental science, or a closely related field. Must hold a position at a recognised university or research institution. Cannot be engaged by Consciobite or any listed manufacturer as a consultant, advisor, or paid researcher during their term.

_Suggested first appointment:_ A faculty member from a university food science or sustainability department — UCD, TCD, or UCC in an Irish context; any Russell Group or Ivy League equivalent elsewhere.

### 3.2 Regulatory / Civil Society Member
A current or recently retired civil servant, public health official, or consumer protection officer with relevant domain experience. In Ireland, this would ideally be someone with a background in the Food Safety Authority of Ireland (FSAI), the EPA, or the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC). Could alternatively be a senior figure from a consumer advocacy NGO such as Which? or the Consumers' Association.

_Why this matters:_ This seat gives regulators a trusted peer inside the process. When Consciobite approaches the FSAI or DAFM, the regulator already knows the governance structure is credible because someone from their world vouches for it.

### 3.3 Industry Member (non-client)
A senior professional from the food or retail sector who is **not** a paying client of Consciobite and has no commercial interest in the scores of any listed product. A suitable candidate might be a retired category director from a supermarket chain, a supply-chain sustainability lead from a manufacturer that has explicitly chosen not to list on Consciobite, or a trade association officer.

_Why this matters:_ This seat prevents the Panel from being dismissed as purely academic. Industry credibility makes the governance story more durable in retailer pitches.

### 3.4 Quorum and Chair
A quorum requires all three members. The Chair rotates annually between members. The Chair signs off on published audit reports.

---

## 4. Mandate

The Panel has four explicit powers and one explicit limit.

### 4.1 What the Panel Can Do

**Methodology audit.** At least once per year, the Panel reviews the complete GreenGrade algorithm: emission factors, data sources, weighting of the seven supply-chain categories, and any changes made since the last audit. It may request the underlying academic references and data provenance records at any time.

**Score challenge.** Any Panel member may trigger a review of a specific product score if they have reason to believe it is inconsistent with the published methodology. Consciobite must respond within 14 days with a full explanation. The Panel's conclusion is published.

**Conflict-of-interest flag.** If the Panel identifies a commercial arrangement that it believes creates a risk of score manipulation, it may issue a formal notice to Consciobite's board. Consciobite must respond publicly within 30 days. If the Panel is not satisfied with the response, it may publish a dissent.

**Annual public report.** The Panel publishes a short annual statement (target: 500–800 words) summarising what it reviewed, what it found, and whether the GreenGrade methodology continues to meet the independence standard. This is posted on the Consciobite website and on each Panel member's institutional page.

### 4.2 What the Panel Cannot Do

The Panel does **not** set commercial pricing, decide which manufacturers are accepted onto the platform, or direct product development. It has no executive authority. Its role is purely to audit and to report.

---

## 5. Conflict-of-Interest Firewall

The following rules apply to all Panel members for the duration of their term and for 12 months after leaving the Panel.

- No paid or unpaid advisory relationship with Consciobite.
- No financial interest in any company that is a paying client of Consciobite.
- No participation in any Panel review of a product made by a company in which they hold a personal interest.
- Members must disclose any new potential conflict within 14 days of it arising. The disclosure is recorded and published in the next annual report.

If a conflict cannot be resolved, the member resigns and Consciobite appoints a replacement within 60 days.

---

## 6. Access Rights

Consciobite grants Panel members read-only access to:

- The full GreenGrade algorithm and all revision history
- The audit trail database (who reviewed what score, when, and why)
- The list of paying clients and their listing agreements (under NDA)
- All data provenance citations for emission factors

Panel members do not have access to individual user data, unpublished product data, or commercially sensitive manufacturing information beyond what is necessary to audit a specific score.

---

## 7. Meetings and Cadence

| Activity | Frequency | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Full Panel meeting | Twice yearly (June and December) | Video call, 90 minutes |
| Methodology audit | Annual (December meeting) | Async review + call |
| Score challenge reviews | As triggered | Async, within 14 days |
| Annual public report | January each year | Written, published online |
| Emergency session | As needed | Called by any member with 7 days notice |

Meeting notes are retained internally. The annual report is the only document published externally, except for formal dissents under Section 4.1.

---

## 8. Compensation and Expenses

Panel members serve in a voluntary capacity. Consciobite covers reasonable travel and accommodation expenses for any in-person meetings, if held. No fee is paid for service.

_Rationale:_ Payment from Consciobite to Panel members would itself create a dependency. Voluntary service is standard practice for methodology oversight bodies (cf. the Advertising Standards Authority, Which? policy panels). The academic and civil society members typically log this as professional service.

---

## 9. Term and Renewal

Each member serves a two-year term, renewable once (maximum four years continuous service). Terms are staggered so that no more than two seats turn over in the same year, preserving institutional memory.

Founding members serve staggered initial terms:
- Academic Member: 2 years
- Regulatory Member: 3 years (extended first term to stagger)
- Industry Member: 2 years

After the founding cohort, all terms are two years, renewable once.

## 10. How to Talk About This

### In a regulator pitch (FSAI, EPA, DAFM)

> "We recognised early that charging manufacturers for listing creates a structural tension with our independence claims. So before we scaled, we established a three-person Independent Advisory Panel — an academic, a former civil servant with FSAI/EPA experience, and a non-client industry figure. They have read-only access to our scoring algorithm, our audit trail, and our client list. They publish an annual statement. We'd welcome your feedback on the panel composition or its terms of reference."

### In a retailer pitch (Tesco, Lidl, M&S)

> "The GreenGrade score is independently audited. We have a Panel — academic, ex-regulator, non-client industry — that reviews our methodology annually and can challenge any individual score. Their findings are published. This means the score on your shelf label carries independent verification, not just our word."

### In a media interview or investor Q&A

> "The obvious question is: you charge manufacturers, so aren't you just selling high scores? Our answer is governance. Our scoring methodology is audited by an independent panel that has no commercial relationship with us or our clients. They can flag any score they believe is inconsistent with the science, and we have to respond publicly. It's the same model that ratings agencies and testing labs use to protect their credibility — and it's the reason retailers and regulators take them seriously."

## Appendix A: Precedents

These organisations use similar independent oversight structures for methodology credibility:

- **Nutri-Score Scientific Committee** — independent academic body that governs the EU's front-of-pack nutrition scoring system
- **Rainforest Alliance Verification** — third-party auditors review compliance with their sustainability standard
- **Carbon Trust Standard** — independent verification of organisational carbon footprint claims
- **Which? Technical Panel** — volunteer expert panel that reviews product testing methodology

None of these are large or expensive. All of them transformed "trust us" into a credible governance story.
